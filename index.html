The problem

A group holiday is a logistics project disguised as a rest.

Everything starts in a group chat, moves to a spreadsheet nobody opens, and ends with someone stranded at the station. These are the four questions that get asked every single time.

  • ?

    “Which room am I in again?”

    Asked on arrival, at 11pm, by the person whose room was given away three messages ago. Beds get double-booked and someone ends up on the sofa.

  • ?

    “Wait — is anyone picking up Clémentine tonight?”

    Her train time was posted in the chat four days ago, above sixty messages about aperitifs. Nobody volunteered because nobody was asked directly.

  • ?

    “How many are we for dinner on Thursday?”

    Two people leave Wednesday, three arrive Thursday afternoon and one couple counts as two. Nobody can answer without scrolling back a week.

  • ?

    “Do we all have to move rooms mid-week?”

    With staggered arrivals the naive plan makes half the group repack on Thursday. A better plan exists — it is just tedious to find by hand.

Notice what these have in common: one person is expected to hold all of it in their head. That is the mental load, and getting rid of it is the whole point of Kikouchou — the plan lives somewhere everyone can see, and each guest carries their own piece of it.

How it works

Three steps, and the organiser stops being a switchboard.

The point is that you do not fill everything in yourself. The work gets shared out rather than stacked on the organiser: you set up the house, and your friends add their own arrival times and claim their own beds.

  1. Create the trip

    Name, dates, location, and the rooms with how many beds each one has. Two minutes. Renting the same gîte as last year? Import it and the rooms come with it.

    • Pin the house on a map
    • Rooms, tents, caravans, sofa beds
    • Notes, house rules, your Tricount link

  2. Share one link

    Drop the invite link in the group chat, or show the QR code across the table. That is the last logistics message you have to send.

    • Link or QR code
    • Each guest signs up once, in a tap
    • Works on any phone browser

  3. Everyone fills in their own part

    A guest opens the link and walks a short wizard: create an account, say which one they are, claim a room, add their travel times and whether they need a lift. Then the calendar is simply correct.

    • Rooms show live availability
    • Warnings when a room is tight, never walls
    • Changes appear on everyone’s phone

What is inside

Built for the four things that actually go wrong.

Beds, travel, coordination and connectivity. Everything else in Kikouchou exists to serve one of those.

Rooms & calendar

Every bed, every night, on one board.

Each room has a real bed count, so the app knows when it is full. Drag a guest onto a room to assign them; the occupancy timeline shows the whole week at once and warns you before you overbook a night.

  • Capacity you can see, not a rule you fight. “3 of 4 spots taken”, “Full”, and a warning when a stay would push a room past its bed count — a warning, not a refusal. Three friends squeezing into a double is a decision, not an error.
  • Optimise automatically. One tap reshuffles the week to fit everyone in with the fewest room changes — the puzzle that staggered arrivals create.
  • Month and timeline views. A month grid for the shape of the trip, a per-guest timeline for who is where tonight.
  • Guests tonight. A permanent count of who is actually sleeping in the house on today’s date — the number you need before you cook.

Arrivals, departures & pickups

Nobody gets forgotten at the station.

Every journey is a record: train, plane, car or bus, with a time, a place and a number. Mark it “needs pickup” and it stays on the upcoming-pickups list until somebody’s name is against it.

  • A driver per pickup. Pick who is fetching whom, so “someone should go” becomes a person with a name.
  • Pickups needing a driver. Counted and surfaced, because an unclaimed pickup is the one that gets missed.
  • Map view. Stations, airports and the house on one map, with a line from origin to destination for each leg.
  • Flight and train numbers. TGV 8541, terminal 2F — whatever the driver needs to find the right platform.

Sharing & live sync

One link. Eleven people editing. No conflicts.

Everyone works on the same trip at the same time and sees each other’s changes land. Two people editing the same room at once get merged rather than one overwriting the other — no “who has the latest version?”.

  • A guided join. Create an account → which one are you → pick your room → your travel details. Four screens, and their part is done.
  • Presence. “3 people online”, “Everyone is up to date” — you can tell whether the plan you are looking at is the current one.
  • Synced in Europe. The shared copy of your trip lives in a database hosted in Europe, under EU data protection rules — not in whichever region happened to be cheapest.
  • Everyone gets an account. That is what makes a guest’s room and travel times follow them between devices and show up live for everyone else. Planning a trip on your own still needs nothing at all.

Offline-first

The gîte has no signal. The app does not care.

Kikouchou keeps the whole trip on your device and only talks to a server to sync. In a stone farmhouse, on a train, in an airport queue with two bars — you can still read the plan and change it. Your edits queue up and land when you have signal again.

  • Read and write with no connection. Not a read-only cache: you can assign rooms and add trains offline.
  • An honest queue. “2 changes not sent yet” instead of a spinner and a silent failure.
  • Install it like an app. Add it to your home screen and it opens full-screen, from an icon, with no browser chrome.
  • Offline maps. Map tiles you have already looked at are cached, so the station is still on the map in the dead zone.

And the rest

The details that make a week actually work.

  • An agenda people sign up to

    Walks, markets, a visit to the château, a shared paella. Add an activity with a seat limit and guests join or leave themselves, so you know it is six for the hike before you book.

  • Guests, not just names

    A colour each, their own stay dates, and a headcount — “Alice + Julie” counts as two for beds and for dinner. Notes carry the things hosts forget: vegan, allergic to cats, arriving with a dog.

  • An assistant that runs on your phone

    Ask “who is staying tonight?” or say “add a guest named Margerite” in plain language. The model is downloaded once and runs on your own device — your trip is never sent to an AI provider.

  • Totals at a glance

    People, rooms, assignments, transport legs, and how many pickups still have no driver — per trip or across every trip you have ever organised.

  • French and English

    The whole app in both languages, with dates formatted the way each one expects. Light and dark themes, and layouts that work from a phone to a laptop.

  • Reuse last year’s house

    Type a location you have used before and Kikouchou offers to import it: same rooms, same notes, new dates. The annual trip takes a minute to set up.

Use cases

If several people sleep somewhere that is not theirs, this helps.

The pattern is always the same — fixed beds, moving people, someone arriving by train. Here is what that looks like in practice.

  • 🏖️

    The summer house with friends

    10–14 people · 1 week · staggered arrivals

    The hard part
    Half the group takes the whole fortnight, half comes for a long weekend in the middle. Every arrival changes who can sleep where.
    With Kikouchou
    Each guest sets their own stay dates, then Optimise automatically lays out the week with as few room moves as possible. The timeline shows the handovers.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

    The family reunion

    3 generations · a house that is not big enough

    The hard part
    Couples need doubles, cousins can share a bunk room, grandparents need the ground floor, and someone has to sleep in the caravan.
    With Kikouchou
    Rooms can be a bedroom, a tent, a caravan or a sofa bed, each with its own bed count. Headcounts keep couples together and the totals honest.
  • 🎿

    The ski chalet on rotation

    2 weeks · overlapping weekends

    The hard part
    People swap in and out every Saturday, beds turn over the same day, and the chalet has patchy 3G at best.
    With Kikouchou
    The occupancy timeline makes same-day turnovers visible, and the app keeps working in the valley — your changes sync when you get back to the village.
  • 💍

    The wedding weekend gîte

    20+ guests · everyone arrives at once

    The hard part
    A dozen trains and flights land on the Friday, into a station 40 minutes away, and the couple getting married should not be running a taxi service.
    With Kikouchou
    Guests enter their own arrivals. The pickup list groups them by time so two people can be collected in one run, and each run has a named driver.
  • 🏄

    The surf, climbing or hiking trip

    Vans, tents and a bunkhouse

    The hard part
    Sleeping spots are improvised, plans change with the weather, and half the group is out of signal for most of the day.
    With Kikouchou
    Tents and vans are rooms like any other. The agenda holds the day’s plan with sign-ups, and everything is editable offline from a car park at the trailhead.
  • 💻

    The team offsite or retreat

    A rented villa · 3 days · one organiser

    The hard part
    One person is fielding room preferences, dietary requirements and flight times in DMs, and re-sending the same summary five times.
    With Kikouchou
    Share the link once. Everyone claims a room, adds their flights and their dietary note themselves, and the plan is always the current one.

Your data

Local first, European when it is shared.

Most planning apps put your data on their server and lend it back to you. Kikouchou starts the other way round: a trip you plan on your own never leaves your device. Share it, and it syncs through a database hosted in Europe — because a plan several people edit has to live somewhere they can all reach.

That is what makes it work on a train, and it is why there is no account wall in front of you when you start. Kikouchou is open source under the MIT licence — the code is on GitHub, so you can read exactly what it stores, where it goes, and what never leaves your device at all.

Read the source
  • It starts on your device

    A new trip is stored in your browser’s own database. No account, no upload, nothing to agree to.

  • Shared trips sync in Europe

    The moment you invite someone, the shared copy lives in a database hosted in Europe and covered by EU data protection rules.

  • An account is for sharing, not for using

    Plan an entire trip without one. Signing up is what lets the people you invite see your room and your travel times, and edit the plan alongside you.

  • The AI stays on your device too

    The assistant’s model is downloaded to your browser and runs there. Your guests, rooms and travel times are not sent to any AI service.

  • Yours to delete

    One button clears everything the app has stored on this device, permanently.

Install it

Put it on your home screen in twenty seconds.

Kikouchou is a progressive web app: there is nothing in an app store, and no download to approve. Add it from your browser and it behaves like a native app — its own icon, full screen, and it opens instantly even with no signal.

  • iPhone & iPad · Safari

    1. Open app.kikouchou.app in Safari
    2. Tap the Share button in the toolbar
    3. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add

  • Android · Chrome

    1. Open app.kikouchou.app in Chrome
    2. Accept the Install app prompt if it appears
    3. Otherwise: menu ⋮ → Add to home screen

  • Mac, Windows & Linux

    1. Open app.kikouchou.app in Chrome or Edge
    2. Click the install icon in the address bar
    3. It gets its own window and dock icon

Installing is optional — everything works in a normal browser tab as well.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

Do my friends need to create an account?
Yes — it is the first step when they open your invite link, and it takes a tap. That account is what keeps their room and their travel times attached to them across devices, and what lets the change show up live on everyone else’s phone. You do not need one to start planning a trip on your own.
Is it really free?
Yes, and there is no paid tier waiting behind a feature. It is a personal open source project under the MIT licence, so you can also run your own copy if you would rather.
Does it work without internet?
Yes. After the first load, the app and your trips are on your device. You can read and edit everything offline; changes queue up and are sent the next time you have a connection. Map tiles you have already viewed are cached too.
Where is my data stored?
In your browser, in a local database on your device. A trip you keep to yourself stays there. Once you share one, the shared copy also lives in a database hosted in Europe, so the people you invited can read and edit it.
What if two of us end up in the same bed?
The board tells you: it shows how many spots are taken and warns you when a stay would push a room past its bed count. Then it lets you go ahead anyway — sharing a double, or throwing a mattress on the floor, is a normal holiday decision rather than a data error. And when two edits genuinely cross in mid-air they are merged, instead of one overwriting the other.
Does it split the money, or plan the meals?
Not today — those are on the ideas list, not in the app. For now, paste your Tricount or Splitwise link into the trip description, where everyone will find it. Headcounts and “guests tonight” at least give you the right number of plates.
Which languages and devices does it support?
French and English, with light and dark themes. It runs in any modern browser on a phone, tablet, laptop or desktop. The on-device assistant additionally needs WebGPU, so it works on recent hardware and quietly stays out of the way on older devices.
Can I reuse the same house next year?
Yes. Start typing a location you have used before and Kikouchou offers to import that trip: the rooms, the location and the notes are copied to your new dates.
